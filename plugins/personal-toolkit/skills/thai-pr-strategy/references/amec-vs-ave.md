# AMEC Integrated Evaluation Framework vs. AVE

## The problem this solves

The survey found Thai PR pros are digital-first and AI-using in their
day-to-day work, but most still report and get evaluated on legacy metrics:
raw Output counts (number of placements, views, likes, shares) and AVE
(Advertising Value Equivalency — converting earned media into an equivalent
ad-spend dollar figure). AVE is widely discredited internationally (it's
explicitly rejected by the Barcelona Principles) but persists in Thailand
because of habit and because off-the-shelf clipping/monitoring tools default
to it.

AMEC (the International Association for Measurement and Evaluation of
Communication) frameworks are the standard most of the world's PR industry
has already shifted to. Use AMEC's four-layer model instead.

## The four layers

1. **Output** — what you produced/got placed. Number of media placements,
   views, likes, shares, press releases sent. *Necessary but not sufficient*
   — this is activity, not effect.
2. **Outtake** — what the audience took in. Did the target audience notice,
   understand, recall the message? Comprehension, message recall,
   awareness lift.
3. **Outcome** — behavioral/attitudinal change. Did attitudes shift? Did
   confidence in the brand increase? Did people decide to purchase/try the
   product?
4. **Impact** — organizational-level effect, long-term. Sales lift,
   reputation/image improvement, share price, customer lifetime value,
   crisis containment.

The further down this list, the harder to measure but the more it actually
matters to the business. A mature PR measurement plan reports on at least
one metric from each layer, not just Output.

## Worked example: KPI tree for a product-launch PR campaign

- **Output:** 25 media placements, 40 KOL posts, 500K total reach
- **Outtake:** message recall in post-campaign survey (target: 60%+ recall
  of core claim), share-of-voice vs. category
- **Outcome:** purchase intent lift (pre/post survey), branded search volume
  lift, sentiment shift (% positive in social listening)
- **Impact:** incremental sales attributable to campaign window, brand
  trust score movement in next brand-health tracker wave

## Talking points for justifying the switch

Use these when a client, finance team, or boss is attached to AVE/Output
reporting:

- "AVE tells you what the coverage would have cost as an ad — it doesn't
  tell you whether it worked. We can keep tracking Output for activity
  reporting, but the KPI we're accountable to should be an Outcome or
  Impact metric tied to what the business actually cares about."
- "International measurement standards (AMEC, the Barcelona Principles)
  dropped AVE years ago specifically because it incentivizes volume over
  effect — exactly the failure mode this research found in 74%+ of
  digital-first Thai practitioners."
- "This isn't a harder report to produce — it's mostly the same data
  (placements, reach) plus one or two outcome signals you likely already
  have access to (search lift, sentiment, survey data, sales data)."
- Per this research, expect AMEC + outcome-based KPIs to become a default
  contractual/hiring requirement within roughly 3 years as AVE's role
  declines — positioning now is a competitive advantage, not just a
  best-practice nicety.

## PESO Model (use alongside AMEC for channel planning, not measurement)

- **Paid** — media you buy (ads, boosted posts, sponsored placements)
- **Earned** — coverage you didn't pay for (press, KOL/influencer mentions
  driven by genuine interest)
- **Shared** — organic spread on social platforms (shares, reposts, UGC)
- **Owned** — channels the brand controls directly (website, brand social
  accounts, LINE OA, email/CRM)

The research found Thai practitioners already blend all four in practice —
but a meaningful share, especially in government communications, don't
recognize the PESO terminology even while doing it. When advising, it's fine
to introduce PESO as a naming convention for what they're already doing,
not as a new requirement.
