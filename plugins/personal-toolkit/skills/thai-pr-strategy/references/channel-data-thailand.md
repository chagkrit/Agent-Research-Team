# Channel Effectiveness Data (Thailand, 2026 survey)

Two different rankings came out of the research — don't conflate them.

## Most *influential* channels (shape perception/credibility)

In descending order of influence on PR outcomes:

1. Brand Social Media (owned)
2. Influencer / KOL
3. Micro-Communities (niche forums, closed groups, fan communities)
4. Online News
5. Traditional Media

## Most *successful in practice* channels (where campaigns actually land)

1. Facebook
2. TikTok
3. Website (owned)
4. Instagram
5. LINE

Note the asymmetry: traditional/online news ranks as influential
(credibility-conferring) but doesn't rank in the top channels for practical
campaign success — meanwhile LINE, a channel absent from the "influence"
list, ranks among the most successful in practice. Read this as: news media
still matters for legitimacy and third-party validation, but the actual
reach/engagement work is happening on social + owned digital channels.

## Caveat — this is not a universal ranking

The research is explicit that the right channel mix depends on brand and
product category. Use this list as the Thai-market base rate, then adjust:
B2B vs. B2C, regulated industries (pharma, finance) vs. consumer goods,
mass-market vs. niche/premium will each shift the mix. Don't hand someone a
generic "do Facebook + TikTok" answer without asking what they're selling
and to whom.

## Press releases are not dead

Despite the digital shift, traditional press releases remain in active,
preferred use by both PR professionals and journalists in this market.
Don't recommend dropping press releases entirely in favor of social-only
strategy — the "Slow PR" trend (see nine-trends.md) is about cutting
*unnecessary low-value* releases, not eliminating the format.

## Influencer / KOC / Founder trade-off

- **Influencers/KOLs:** best lever for short-term reach and fast
  awareness spikes. Weak on durable trust by themselves.
- **Durable credibility** requires layering in:
  - Content consistency over time (not one-off bursts)
  - Fact-referenced content (claims backed by data/sources)
  - KOC (Key Opinion Consumer) reviews — real, non-paid-seeming consumer
    voices
  - Continued presence from journalists and influencers, not just a launch
    moment
- **Founder/CEO-led communication** stood out in the research as
  disproportionately effective for PR outcomes specifically — leadership
  speaking directly to the public, tied to the product/service. Recommend
  this as a serious lever, not a vanity add-on, especially for brands
  trying to differentiate from competitors who all look the same on paid
  influencer reach.

Practical recommendation pattern: influencer/KOL for the reach spike +
KOC/fact-based owned content for the trust layer + founder-led moments for
differentiation, rather than picking just one lane.
