# Risk-of-bias / quality assessment tools by study design

Match the tool to the study design being appraised (Step 17). Two independent reviewers score
every included study; a third resolves disagreement.

## RoB 2 (Cochrane Risk of Bias tool, version 2) — randomized controlled trials

Evaluates five domains, each rated low / some concerns / high risk of bias:
1. Bias arising from the randomization process
2. Bias due to deviations from intended interventions
3. Bias due to missing outcome data
4. Bias in measurement of the outcome
5. Bias in selection of the reported result

Overall judgment is driven by the worst-rated domain, not an average.

## Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) — cohort and case-control (prospective observational) studies

Star system, maximum 9 stars, across three domains:
- **Selection** of participants (up to 4 stars)
- **Comparability** of study groups (up to 2 stars — usually tied to confounder adjustment)
- **Ascertainment** of exposure (case-control) or outcome (cohort) (up to 3 stars)

Interpretation used in this guide: 9 stars = low risk of bias, 7–8 = medium risk, ≤6 = high risk.
(Cutoffs vary by field convention — state which convention is used in the methods section.)

## ROBINS-I — nonrandomized studies of interventions

Extends the RoB 2 logic to nonrandomized intervention studies. Domains: confounding, selection
of participants, classification of interventions, deviations from intended interventions,
missing data, measurement of outcomes, and selection of the reported result. Judged as low,
moderate, serious, or critical risk of bias, with "critical" typically excluding a study from
the synthesis rather than just downweighting it.

## QUADAS-2 — diagnostic accuracy studies

Four domains, each assessed for both risk of bias and applicability to the review question:
patient selection, index test, reference standard, and flow/timing. Signaling questions under
each domain drive the overall risk-of-bias judgment per domain.

## QUIPS (Quality In Prognosis Studies) — prognostic factor studies

Six domains: study participation, study attrition, prognostic factor measurement, outcome
measurement, study confounding, and statistical analysis/reporting. Each rated low/moderate/high
risk of bias.

## PROBAST — prediction model studies

For development, validation, or updating of diagnostic/prognostic prediction models. Four
domains: participants, predictors, outcome, and analysis — each assessed for both risk of bias
and applicability, regardless of medical domain or modeling technique used.

## Reporting

State the tool, the exact judgment scale used, and inter-rater agreement (or the resolution
process) in the methods section. A visual summary — a traffic-light plot (domain × study) or
a stacked bar chart of risk-of-bias distribution — is the conventional way to present this in
the results; it's usually generated from the same Stata/RevMan output used for the
meta-analysis, not built separately.
